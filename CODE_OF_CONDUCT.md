# Code of Conduct 🦦

This is the Code of Conduct for OtterLab's GitHub: our repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and anything else we host here. The OtterLab Discord has its own Code of Conduct. If something happens on Discord, the Discord code applies there.

## Our promise

Technology is better when we build it together. This is a welcoming place for people at every level, and especially for beginners. Everyone gets to take part without being harassed, talked down to, or made to feel behind.

## How we work together here

- **Be kind.** Disagree about code, tools and approaches, not about people. Comment on the work, not the person.
- **Don't gatekeep.** No "just Google it", "RTFM", "this is trivial" or "obviously". Explain jargon, point to good resources, and welcome questions.
- **Welcome all kinds of building.** Using AI assistants, no-code tools or copied configs is fine.
- **Be honest about where things came from.** Credit the people, guides and tools that helped you. If code, text or an answer is AI-assisted, say so, and make sure you understand it and have tried it before you submit or recommend it.
- **Review with care.** Say what works before what doesn't. Separate "this must change" from "this is a suggestion". Give the reason, not just the verdict. If you review, you are teaching as much as checking.
- **Take reviews kindly.** Feedback is about the work. Ask if something is unclear. Nobody here expects a perfect first pull request.
- **Be patient.** Maintainers are volunteers and members have different time zones and schedules. A quiet issue is not a rejection.
- **Help the way you would want to be helped.** Be specific about what you tried, what happened and what you expected. Let people learn instead of taking over their work.

## Secrets and privacy

Never commit or post passwords, API keys, tokens, private keys, recovery codes, `.env` files, your home's public IP address, or anyone's private information. That includes issue text, screenshots and logs.

Git keeps history, so deleting a file or a comment does not undo a leak. **If a secret was committed or posted, rotate or revoke it first**, then tell a maintainer so they can help remove it. Nobody gets shamed for a leak. See `SECURITY.md`.

## What we don't allow

- Harassment, insults, hate, slurs, discrimination, threats or sexual content
- Sharing other people's personal information without permission
- Spam, advertising, scams or malware, including in issues, pull requests and project READMEs
- Anything illegal, including pirated or cracked software, and help attacking systems you don't own or have permission to test
- Passing off someone else's work as yours, or submitting work you do not have the right to share
- Continuing after someone has asked you to stop
- Trying to get around a block or a lock on a thread

## Contributions and credit

Authors keep the copyright to what they write, and you must have the right to share what you submit. By submitting, you allow OtterLab to display it in the repository, on the website, on Discord and in social posts, with credit. See `CONTRIBUTING.md` for the details.

## Where and who

This code applies to the OtterLab repositories and organization: issues, pull requests, reviews, comments, discussions, project boards, commit messages, and direct contact that starts from them. It applies to everyone, including maintainers. It also applies to someone representing OtterLab elsewhere.

We may act on behavior outside GitHub when it affects people here, such as harassing a contributor elsewhere, or contacting a maintainer elsewhere to argue a decision or get around a block.

Maintainers keep the space tidy and safe and have the final say on what stays. They will explain decisions when they can, but they do not have to debate them in public.

Please do not use bots, scripts or automation to collect other people's personal information from our repositories, or to flood issues or pull requests.

## Patterns count

A single small slip is usually just a slip. But a steady run of comments that are each almost fine, and together make people feel unwelcome, is a problem too. Maintainers may act on a pattern even when no single comment would have been enough.

## Reporting

If you see or experience something that breaks this code, tell a maintainer privately. Please do not open a public issue about it.

- Email **conduct@otterlab.click** with a link to the issue, pull request or comment, who was involved, what happened, and whether it is still going on.
- You can also use GitHub's "Report content" option on a comment, or "Report" on a user.

Reports are handled privately and taken seriously, and we do not reveal who reported. Nobody will be penalized for a good-faith report, even if it turns out there was no violation. We aim to acknowledge a report within 24 hours on weekdays and within 3 days at the latest. This is a small volunteer project, so it may sometimes take longer, and we will say so. If someone may be in danger, say so in your report and contact emergency services if the danger is immediate.

**If the report is about a maintainer.** A maintainer does not decide a report about themselves, a close friend, or someone they work with. Email conduct@otterlab.click and say that the report concerns a maintainer. An independent reviewer, a community member who has no tie to the people involved, reviews the report and decides what happens. To find one, a moderator posts a short call for a volunteer in `#general` that says only that a review is needed. It does not name anyone or describe the report. If nobody volunteers within 24 hours, a reviewer is chosen at random from members who were active in the community in the last 24 hours. For the draw, a moderator uses a bot to pick one member at random from the "Recently Active" role, which a bot keeps to members who posted or joined voice in the last 24 hours. If the member drawn has a tie to the people involved, or declines, the draw is repeated. The moderator then posts how many members held the role, how many draws it took, and who accepted, without the report details, so the pick can be checked. The reviewer keeps the report confidential and is told what to do with it.

## What happens next

Maintainers aim for the lightest step that works, and escalate for repeated problems.

| Level | Examples | What may happen |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Minor | An honest mistake, a first slip, an unclear case, a committed secret | A friendly reply or a private note, and help fixing it |
| 2. Moderate | A repeated minor issue, dismissive or gatekeeping reviews after a note, spam | A clear written warning, and the comment hidden or removed |
| 3. Serious | Ongoing disruption, personal attacks, ignoring a warning | Locking a thread, or a temporary block from the repository or organization (GitHub offers 1, 3, 7 or 30 days) |
| 4. Severe | Harassment, threats, sharing someone's private information, scams, malware, anything illegal, unwanted sexual content | An immediate permanent block. Where it applies, we may report it to GitHub, other communities or the authorities |

Repeated problems move up a level. Level 4 skips the earlier steps. Maintainers may remove content at any time. Moderation notes are written plainly: what happened, what is needed and what happens next.

## Appeals

Email conduct@otterlab.click within 14 days of a level 2, 3 or 4 action. Another maintainer takes a second look when there is one, and replies within 7 days. One appeal per action. Level 4 blocks are rarely reversed.

## Changes

We may update this code. Changes are announced in the repository, and in the organization's profile README when there is one. Small edits take effect after 7 days, and major changes after 30 days.
